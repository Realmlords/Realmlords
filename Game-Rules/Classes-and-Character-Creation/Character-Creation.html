    Character Creation

    Character Creation

    Creating a character requires generating the basic abilities of your adventurer, understanding their cultural and upbringings, choosing a class, defining their allies, and preparing for adventure.

    During character creation you may accrue blessings of Chaos for embracing serendipity, keep track of the total number of these. At the end of character creation each will influence their life in interesting ways.

    1. Roll characteristics
    2. Culture + Background
    3. Class
    4. Class kit
    5. Calculate HP and other attributes
    6. Starting gear
    7. Name, backstory

    Roll Characteristics

    Characters have six Characteristics: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Wisdom, Intelligence, and Charisma - usually between 3 and 18. These reflect their general potency in a number of domains. In addition to adding to rolls with associated skills these characteristics also:

    • Strength: melee attack and damage rolls, carrying capacity
    • Dexterity: armor class, ranged attack rolls, avoiding fireballs
    • Constitution: hit points, saves against poison and transformation effects
    • Intelligence: soul points, bonus training points, additional languages
    • Wisdom: saves against mental and magical effects, avoiding surprise and ambush, teaching, spiritual armor class
    • Charisma: reaction rolls, Command Capacity

    Roll a set of 3d6 four times in a row and write down these numbers. From here you have a choice:

    • Commit to rolling two more sets of 3d6, assign each of these six numbers to your six characteristics in any order, gain 2 blessings of Chaos.
    • Ignore the prior rolls and use a static array. Assign 15, 12, 10, 10, 9, 7 to your characteristics.

    Each has a corresponding modifier, a derived value used when that attribute is relevant to a die roll.

    Culture and Background

    • Gain 2 training points in each of your culture’s favored skills, these will be used to determine your total Skill Rank later, write down any (specializations) from cultural skills
    • Write down your starting languages next to the Language skill
    • Note any additional traits
    • Determine your character’s exact birthplace from the options presented, either by roll or choice.

    After this you may either choose your family profession, or roll on your culture’s background chart to gain another blessing of Chaos. Note down how your character was raised and what skills they gain from this. This does not have to favor one parent or another, and can also include induction into a fraternal organization, adoption by another clan, or other manner of relationship.

    Gain 6 Training Points in each of the skills your family practices.

    Beast Kingdoms

    • Languages: Noctian
    • Gain specialization in Beastmastery (Hybrid Animals)
    • Head is that of an animal, see Animal Aspect below
    • Choose or roll to be born in: 1 - the domain of grand Chimericon under Prince Cyrus, 2 - the lands of radical reformist Prince Darius and his Lankhian consort, 3 - the ailing princedom of old Hystaspes, 4 - the small vibrant holdings of Princess Erina

    These kingdoms are a contentious group of fiefdoms held together by a rigid caste system, where people have the heads of animals. Long ago the people of the Beast Kingdoms lived deep within Noctis Labyrinthus, until the coming of ash and the death of the Fates drove them out. What remains in Noctis is strange and twisted.

    The princes hold near absolute power within their domains, and life is harshly dictated by numerous taboos; forbidding the eating of certain foods, private possession of gold, the use of many colors of dress by the lower classes, and in all ways circumscribing life on all fronts.

    Gain 6 training points based on your family’s caste and profession. Animals are listed in order of their approximate ranking in society.

    New Lankh

    • Languages: Mortanian
    • 2 training points in Alchemy, Devicecraft, Stealth, Exotic Arms
    • Maintenance of cost of Lankh technology is reduced by 1
    • Specialization in Devicecraft with (Lankh Technology), and Stealth (Urban Environments)
    • Choose or roll to be born: 1 - under the shadow of the Inquisitorial tower, 2 - in the former Alchemist’s quarter, 3 - among the raucous thespians and many byways of Low Street, 4 - outside the city proper, beyond the Dome of Preservation

    New Lankh is a soot-filled city which teleported from the Wandering Moon twenty years ago, home to technological marvels that are slowly breaking down in their new environment. Currently a vast spell, known as the Dome of Preservation, protects the city from the effects of this degradation, and also wreaths it in a continual haze.

    Previously Lankh was merely a district of a sprawling gaslit metropolis, ruled by vampiric overlords. Now its position in an entirely new world is much more uncertain. After their arrival the people of Lankh fought a number of wars against their new neighbors. Some way they emerged victorious since neither the Beast Kingdoms or the Alcherans could agree on who deserved to conquer such a vital opportunity, others say it was the diseases the city brought with it. Currently ruled by the High Inspectrix, the chief of police that happened to be teleported along with the city. Looming most uncertainly is what will happen upon the next conjunction with the Wandering Moon.

    Blemmyes

    • Languages: Blem, Ophidian
    • 2 training points in Bushcraft, Heal, Lore, Investigation
    • Additional specialization in Lore with (History of the Blem)
    • Headless Resiliency: when rolling for Doom roll twice and choose either result
    • Choose or roll to be born: 1, 2 - among the Alcherans in the City of Poison, 2 - near the Ophidian exiles on Serpent Island, 3 - in the hills north of the Beast Kingdoms

    Headless men, also called the akephaloi, they have large faces on their chests rather than the typical arrangement. Most live in and around Blem, the City of Poison, although some have migrated through Alcheran lands. The Blemmyes claim descent from the original inhabitants of the Shrinelands, having survived the floods of the First Age, corrupting sands of the Third Age, and all number of wars, plagues, and disasters. Many hope one day to become worth of returning to their ancient homeland - the utopian city of Alkesh.

    Ophidian

    • Languages: Ophidian
    • 2 training points in Alchemy, Diplomacy, Language, Seneschal
    • Specialization in (Poison) with Alchemy, and (Daggers) in Hand Weapon
    • Toxic blood, ability to draw out poison

    The height of the Ophidian Empire has long gone, and recent years have added further strife. Civil wars rage across what was once the empire, resulting in a group of Ophidian exiles occupying the Serpent Island. Ophidians themselves have a varying degree of serpentine traits. Most slither on a serpentine tail, while all of them have poison blood and fangs. The War of Four Empresses has shaken some faith in the Serpent God, with some seeking to restore more ancient understandings - while others seek new ways.

    City of Steel

    • Languages: Underspeech, Alcheran
    • 2 training points in Artisanry, Devicecraft, Mining, Hand Weapon, and Enchantment
    • For both Great and Hand Weapons have a specialization in fighting with (Hammers)
    • Specialization with (tunneling) in Mining, and (Earth) in Enchantment
    • Cave sense: able to navigate in enclosed environments without light

    The twin City of Steel and City of Miners has a distinct history harkening back to dwarven holds from long ago. From this they still retain their traditions, and the ability to thrive underground. In the time since they have become more akin to their Alcheran neighbors as one of the great city-states.

    Alongside the gods the people of the City of Steel revere their ancestors, constructing elaborate tombs to honor these forebears. Greatest among them is their mythical founder-hero, the Lawgiver, from whom their strong traditions of honor and hospitality are derived. Tragically however, his tomb stands empty. Legends tell that in ages past the Lawgiver was slain in battle, and buried nameless in a mountain pass by contemporaries who did not understand the wisdom he brought to the world. Many citizens will go on quests to seek his remains, but in the meantime the empty tomb also serves as a grand center of the community.

Along these thrive the people of Xanthe, peerless sailors and travelers. They live in wandering clan-ships, variously warring with rivals or marrying friends. Greatest among them are their elders, those who have survived a terrible transformation to become guides for their people - however in recent times a corrupt king has taken power of exiled many of the formerly esteemed elders.

    Time of Birth

    The adventure begins in the year 320. Choose what year your character was born in, and the wind that presided over their birth. For those born in a “short year”, anytime between the year 300 and 278, these are:

    • Zephyrus, law season, a temperate season with many festivals
    • Boreas, monsoon season, a time of potent storms and torrential rain
    • Eurus, chaos season, the most powerful season of vibrant growth and unprecedented change
    • Notus, dry season, a dangerous time of oppressive heat and war

    For those born in the year 301 or after their birth occurred during a long year. This is the result of the Wandering Moon, a sister planet which arrives every 22 years. Each convergence results in an alteration between short years and long years. Long years proceed slower, having more days and an additional season: a cold and cursed time called Winter - the still season.

    Choose Class

    Choose what class your character is.

    The classes are fighting-men, men of many ways, and magic-users. Fighting-Men are masters at arms, and able to lead with great skill. Men of Many Ways gain specializations in all of their skills, and oft find themselves protected by good fortune. Although any dedicated practitioner can learn some sorcery, Magic-Users have a natural affinity for the arcane. Alternatively you may choose two half-classes, as detailed under the rules for Dual-Class characters. Dual-class characters must choose one of their constituent classes to gain a class kit from, see below.

    For your class write down

    • Starting HP, this is the maximum value on the HD + Constitution modifier
    • Saving throws, Evasion includes Dexterity modifier, mental includes Wisdom modifier, physical includes Constitution modifier
    • Special abilities for your class

    Your characters have had some time as adventurers to gain a variety of skills. Presented here are the three basic class kits which you may use. Alternatively you may choose a preset Class Kits for a character with more specific training.

    Fighting-Man Basic Kit

    • Distribute 30 additional training points (maximum of an additional 10 in any single skill)
    • Specialization in weapons of your culture for all Martial skills
    • Heroic understanding of the Mysteries of Martial Training
      • Damage with weapons increased based on martial skill rank, +1, +1d4, +1d6, +1d8…

    Men of Many Ways Basic Kit

    • Distribute 40 additional training points (maximum of any additional 12 in any single skill, ignore bonus from Quick Study during character creation - this is factored in as the additional training points)
    • Choose from
      • Noble - you come from an especially privileged status in society, starting with an additional rank 3 reputation and 1000 coin
      • Priest - heroic mastery of the Priest mystery
      • Alchemist - heroic mastery of Field Alchemy
      • Wanderer - heroic mastery of Bard of the Eastern Wind
      • an additional 10 training points, distributed however you like
      • 1 Attunement in any element

    Magic-User Basic Kit

    • Distribute 6 additional training points to any General or Martial skills (having at least one rank in Lore, Language, and Mathematics recommended)
    • Distribute 12 training points among magical skills
    • Distribute 3 points among your elemental attunements: Air, Earth, Fire, Water, Celestial (maximum 2 in any single element)
    • Heroic aptitude with the Obduration to the Mathematics of Vance

    Begin with some knowledge of the Curriculum, a set of spells developed by the legendary Hebetic wizards. For each magical skill you have and each element you have learned the corresponding spell.     Reputation

    Work with your GM to either create or choose a group which your character has a reputation with, allowing them to call in favors and have as potential allies. This could be the Thief’s Guild of New Lankh, a particular mystery cult, a strong family clan, or perhaps a group of like-minded craftsmen.

    This should be related in some way to your choice of culture, background, or potentially class. Otherwise, a strong family tie to their culture of origin is the most typical. Write this down as a reputation with rank 3 (12 TP), the name of an individual who is your primary contact, and brief description of this relationship.

    Select one other player, decide on how your characters have crossed paths in the past. Gain a rank 2 (6 TP) relationship with the group they are related to. These choices need not be symmetric, someone creating an Alcheran warrior could have aided your work at the Thief’s Guild, gaining them a reputation of 2 among the guild, but your character need not have been to Alchera in return - instead perhaps having spent a year living in exile among the Blem (having chose a different player to create an even deeper web of connections).
