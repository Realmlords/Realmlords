    Skills

    Skills

    Using Skills

    For situations that fall under the ordinary duties of a regular practitioner, no roll is required.
    Additionally, should players come up with a suitable elegant and insightful plan, then it is not the role of the GM to enforce a skill check to sabotage this. However, many moments of tension include the possibility of failure, in this case a skill check is made.

    2d6 + Skill Rank + Characteristic Modifier

    If the result is greater than or equal to the difficulty then the check is a success. Most times the difficulty is known ahead of time unless there are particularly inclement or mysterious circumstances. In some situations success or failure by 2 can have moderating results.

    If you roll doubles then there are additional effects. Odd numbers usually result in a complication, while even numbers offer an opportunity. Double 1’s results in a disaster. On Double 6s your effort is aided by some supernatural power, who will expect to be thanked, consult the Oracle to determine who this is.

    Sometimes a skill roll may be made with either a boon or a bane. These are usually the result of spells, active aid, or supernatural hinderance. When rolling with a boon roll an additional d6 and keep the two highest dice; a bane also rolls an additional die but instead the lower dice are kept. Boons and banes cancel each other out, possibly resulting in a roll with no additional dice. Multiple such effects stack however; so a skill check benefitting from two boons would roll a total of four dice - keeping the two highest.

    Skill Rank

    Track the total number of training points in each skill, these determine a character’s total skill rank. When rolling a skill check the rank is added to the result.

    Colloquially the skill ranks have names. Characters of at least rank 3, experts, may provide aid in the form of a boon in relevant situations. Additionally for skill checks which could reasonably be covered by multiple skills, such as tracking (with bushcraft) a creature you are familiar with (via lore), you gain a +1 bonus to the roll if at least both skills are expert (3) rank.

    Specializations are a focused subdomain of a particular skill. Your character is assumed to have basic competence in this area, and when applicable, your rank is considered one higher. Rogues automatically gain one specialization in each of their general and martial skills, otherwise they may be earned from certain traits or particularly insightful books. Under each skill are a list of example specializations, however these are by no means restrictive.

    Artisanry, Language, Lore, and Exotic Arms are “specialized skills” and operate slightly differently. For each rank a character has in these skills they automatically gain a new specialization, however when operating outside of these areas it is often the case that they are no better off than someone without any ranks in that skill. In essence they are groups of distinct skills united under a single banner.

    General and Special Skills

    Alchemy

    An alchemist may extract useful materials form slain monsters, rare plants, or potent mineral substances. Each creature has a unique usage - which is not initially known to all alchemists - but the process is otherwise a simple one requiring only basic training, tools, and a few minutes of work. At the most basic this can create vials of venom from serpents, but also could include distilling its counterfactor from the blood of a mongoose. Unless you choose to use a live snake or engage in other such unwise practices there is usually no chance of failure for this process.

    Alchemical goods will become spoiled, inert, hazardous, or otherwise unusable unless a skilled alchemist keeps them properly. A character with alchemist’s tools may maintain a number of such items equal to twice their ranks in Alchemy plus their Intelligence modifier.

    Additionally the alchemist may labor over the course of a season on more complex formulae. Doing so requires a recipe and the appropriate ingredients. For more details see Alchemy

    Specializations: bombs, oils, elixirs, poisons

    Artisanry

    Aptitude with craftsmanship, including the fabrication and appraisal of such goods. As a specialized skill a character automatically gains a specialization for each rank. However, the skill itself is not as broadly applicable - an artisan working outside their area of expertise will not be able to apply their skill rank to such efforts.

    Used for crafting.

    DifficultyExample
    7Simple repairs in the field
    13Repair an exceedingly delicate object while travelling

    Specializations: clockwork, jewelry, masonry, weaponsmith, armorsmith, woodwork, glasswork, weaver

    Athletics

    Bend bars, lift gates, and other feats of bodily prowess and Strength. When used with Dexterity one can leap between rooftops or balance on a wave-tossed ship. Used also when fleeing from battle, without a cohesive strategy to retreat it becomes a contest of either Constitution or Dexterity for direct pursuit.

    DifficultyExample
    7Break down a wooden door
    10Walk a tightrope
    13Swim across a perilous river
    - DRTake a penalty equal to armor’s damage reduction for tests of balance or flexibility

    In the context of tactical combat a character may freely ignore such obstacles as chasms or difficult terrain for a number of paces equal to their ranks in Athletics, minus any damage reduction from armor.

    Specializations: balance, climb, jump, marathon, swim

    Bushcraft

    The ability to survive in the wilderness. Includes creating basic shelters, setting snares, rigging a travois, and following tracks.

    Devicecraft

    Aptitude at finding traps, bypassing locks, setting pitfalls, and minor acts of legerdemain and dexterity. Much of the modern use of devicecraft revolves around clockwork from New Lankh. Advanced devices may also require the use of an Artisanry specialization.

    Specializations: trapfinding, pickpocketing, lockpicking

    Diplomacy

    War may be diplomacy by other means, but the diplomat concerns themselves with victory instead of battle. This skill covers understanding the motives of others, and persuading them of your notion of truth. Either through honest or dishonest means. While usually based on Charisma, Wisdom can be used when providing wise and honest counsel in the hopes that your interlocutor will see sense.

    Divination

    Divination is the art of interpreting dreams, signs, and omens from the gods. Alongside passive observation of the world an expert in divination is capable of using cartomancy and haruspexy to pry more intrusively into the plans of She-Who-Lurks.

    As the art of divination is highly subject to the unknown, both player and GM roll 2d6 when making a divination check. The GM then chooses one of these rolls to be the result for what determines the divination. Although initially mysterious there are patterns to which is chosen.

    Specializations: predicting weather, haruspex, averting disaster

    Heal

    Application of the medical arts of both the mind and body. Including diagnostic practices, treatment of diseases, expurgation of poisons, and general palliation. Heal checks do not directly restore hit points, however a trained Healer tending to the wounded increases how many hit points they restore based on their rank. More details are in damage and healing.

    Specializations: autopsy, treating wounds, diseases

    Language

    Skill at speaking the many tongues of the land, as well as reading ancient secrets. Also includes the use of codes and ciphers. All characters learn one additional language per skill rank, written down as a specialization; in addition to automatically starting with their native language.

    A character with at least one rank in both Language and Lore is literate, outside of this the ability to read is not common.

    Lead

    Ability to inspire loyalty in followers, and motivate them to direct action. If the efficacy of an ambush or loyalty of underlings would be in question a Lead check, possibly modified by Wisdom or Charisma addresses this. Notable for increasing your leadership score.

    Specializations: a particular type of follower

    Lore

    DifficultyExample lore
    6Basic familiarity with a phenomena, a good plan to learn more or find a more detailed source later
    8Recall an overview of a complex history
    10Identify a rare monster, recognize a strange essence seep
    14+Descent initial theory when encountering a unique and unprecedented subject

    Knowledge of beasts, phenomenon, and the world at large. All sages have some knowledge of numerous fields, however as a specialized skill characters are entitled to choose one specialization per rank of Lore they have. Within a loremaster’s specialization they always know something, and if a roll is even required, are rolling to see just how deep their knowledge is. If in doubt provide a useful overview then reduce the difficulty by 2. Particularly when monster are involved, hand over a copy of their monster entry. Local variants, strange enchantments, or other complications may still be kept secret - but there is no need to be coy with a character who has specialized lore.

    Suggestions for such specializations are:

    • Realmlore: study of the effects of dominions, auras, and beings from beyond
    • History: heraldry, politics, family lineages, and the origin and purpose of social structures
    • Death: undead, funerary rites, autopsies
    • Architecture
    • Beasts
    • Creatures of Chaos
    • Lords of Law

    Others may be created as needed, however keep in mind that the specific practice and mysteries of other skills are already covered by those. Alchemy already regards substances and their uses, and each magical technique covers the knowledge of spellcraft of that type.

    Martial Skills

    Martial skills represent training with different types of weapons. Most importantly these are added to attack rolls with related weapons. Specializations for martial skills are always a particular weapon within that school. Such masters of the art of combat are able to make use of the mastery properties of that weapon.

    Magical Techniques

    Magical techniques are covered in more detail as their pertain to performing Sorcery. The techniques known to this age are: Adjuration, Alteration, Conjuration, Enchantment, and Thaumaturgy. In addition to determining what spells can be mastered and contributing to spellcasting rolls; a technique check may be called upon to identify or understand a spell being cast or related magical phenomenon.

    Minor Skills

    Some professions are unlikely to be relevant for most adventurers, but are still important for the day-to-day lives of many people in the Shrinelands. In general, adventurers can probably use relevant general skills for most tasks. Skills are meant to be rather broad and powerful. However, in the event more granularity is needed you are free to invent more skills, so long as you carefully consider the negative space you are carving out of the default skill list.

    Where is the perception skill?

    You may have notice there is no skill called something like “awareness”, “spot”, or “notice.” Such circumstances are instead related to a character’s Wisdom score.
